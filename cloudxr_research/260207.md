# Robot simulator alternatives to Isaac Sim for mobile manipulation and imitation learning

**For a mobile manipulator with VR teleoperation via Meta Quest 3S and imitation learning training, the strongest overall stack in 2025 is MuJoCo + LeRobot + Open-Teach (or phospho), supplemented by Gazebo for ROS2 integration.** No single simulator excels at every dimension — VR teleop, IL training, ROS2, and mobile manipulation each have different leaders. MuJoCo dominates imitation learning research (ACT and Diffusion Policy were built on it), has the best physics for manipulation, and integrates natively with LeRobot and the ALOHA ecosystem. OmniGibson offers the most complete VR-to-IL pipeline but inherits Isaac Sim's complexity. Genesis is the most exciting newcomer with 43M FPS speeds, but lacks VR and ROS2 support. The practical path forward combines two or three tools rather than searching for one monolithic solution.

## The simulators that matter most for your use case

Of the 19+ simulators investigated, only a handful genuinely fit the combined requirements of mobile manipulation, VR teleoperation, imitation learning, and ROS2 integration. Here is a focused analysis of the top contenders, followed by coverage of every simulator requested.

### Tier 1: Primary recommendations

**MuJoCo (Google DeepMind)** stands as the **dominant simulator for imitation learning research** in 2024–2025. Open-sourced under Apache 2.0 in 2022, it has exploded in capability. Version 3.4.0 features the best contact physics for manipulation tasks, using generalized coordinates with convex-optimization-based soft contacts. The ALOHA 2 model in MuJoCo Menagerie (50+ curated robots) comes with system-identified parameters. ACT (Action Chunking Transformer) and Diffusion Policy were both originally built and benchmarked on MuJoCo environments. LeRobot natively supports MuJoCo-based environments (gym-aloha, LIBERO, Meta-World). GPU acceleration arrives via **MJX** (JAX-based, millions of steps/sec) and the brand-new **MuJoCo Warp** (March 2025, joint DeepMind+NVIDIA effort claiming >100× faster than MJX for complex scenes). ROS2 integration is maturing through `mujoco_ros2_control` (GSoC 2024, officially listed on ros2_control). The main weakness: **no native VR support** — VR teleoperation requires external tools like Open-Teach or phospho. Rendering is functional (OpenGL + new Madrona GPU batch renderer) but not photorealistic. Installation is trivial: `pip install mujoco`.

**Gazebo (Modern — Harmonic/Ionic/Jetty)** remains the **gold standard for ROS2 integration** and mobile robot simulation. Gazebo Harmonic (LTS, EOL 2028) and Jetty (LTS, EOL 2030) use DART physics by default with OGRE 2.2 PBR rendering. The ros_gz bridge provides mature, bidirectional ROS2 topic mapping. Mobile manipulators are Gazebo's bread and butter — Nav2, MoveIt 2, and ros2_control all have first-class Gazebo support. Thousands of models on Gazebo Fuel. The limitation: **no VR support, no IL framework integration, CPU-only physics**. Gazebo Classic reached EOL January 29, 2025. Gazebo works best as your ROS2 integration and mobile base testing layer, not your IL training environment.

**OmniGibson (Stanford SVL)** offers the **most complete VR teleoperation + imitation learning pipeline** among all simulators. Built on NVIDIA Omniverse/Isaac Sim, it provides RTX ray-traced photorealistic rendering, PhysX GPU physics (rigid bodies, cloth, fluids), and 12 robot models including **4 mobile manipulators** (Fetch, TIAGo, HSR). Its TeleMoMa integration supports Meta Quest controllers, Apple Vision Pro, SpaceMouse, keyboards, and RGB-D body tracking — any combination simultaneously. Built-in `DataCollectionWrapper` and `DataPlaybackWrapper` handle demonstration recording. **ACT, Diffusion Policy, BC-RNN, OpenVLA, and π0 have all been tested with OmniGibson.** The BEHAVIOR-1K benchmark provides 1,000+ household tasks with 10,000 teleoperated trajectories. The critical downside: it inherits Isaac Sim's heavyweight installation, NVIDIA GPU lock-in, and complexity — precisely what you found too difficult. Performance can drop below 10 FPS in large scenes without optimization.

### Tier 2: Strong alternatives with specific strengths

**ManiSkill3 (UCSD/Hillbot)** is the **fastest open-source GPU-parallelized simulator**, achieving **30,000+ FPS** on an RTX 4090 with RGBD observations across thousands of parallel environments. Built on SAPIEN 3 (PhysX 5), it uses **2–3× less GPU memory** than Isaac Lab at equivalent settings. It supports 20+ robot embodiments including mobile manipulators, and ships with baselines for Behavior Cloning, Diffusion Policy, and VLA models (Octo, RDT-1B). Heterogeneous simulation means every parallel environment can have completely different scenes. ManiSkill3 excels at GPU-accelerated IL/RL training but **lacks native VR support and ROS2 integration**. Still in beta (v3.0.0b22), it uses rasterized rendering (not ray-traced). Installation: `pip install mani_skill`.

**Robosuite + Robomimic (Stanford ARISE)** provides the **most mature IL benchmarking ecosystem**. Version 1.5 (October 2024) added humanoid support, composite controllers, and 4 base models for mobile manipulation. Its sister project Robomimic (v0.5.0) natively supports BC, BC-RNN, BC-Transformer, and **Diffusion Policy**, with datasets on HuggingFace. The original Diffusion Policy paper used robosuite as a primary benchmark. MimicGen provides automated demonstration augmentation. Limitations: MuJoCo CPU-only physics (no GPU parallelization), no VR headset support (SpaceMouse/keyboard only), and no native ROS2.

**Isaac Lab (NVIDIA, formerly ORBIT)** is the **structured framework layer** on top of Isaac Sim that may address your usability complaints. Version 2.3 provides ManagerBased and Direct workflow modes, template generators, and growing VR teleoperation support (CloudXR, HTC Vive, Manus gloves). It has **the best photorealistic rendering** (RTX ray tracing) and full ROS2 support. The RTX Pro 6000 is explicitly listed as a "Best" tier GPU. Isaac Lab v2.2+ added LeRobot integration via Isaac Lab-Arena (EnvHub). The new Newton physics engine (co-developed with DeepMind and Disney Research) is being integrated. However, it still requires the full Omniverse stack (12+ GB download, complex dependencies) and uses **2–3× more GPU memory** than ManiSkill3.

## Detailed analysis of every simulator investigated

### Genesis: the disruptive newcomer

Released December 19, 2024, by a consortium of 50+ researchers from CMU, MIT, NVIDIA, and 12+ universities, Genesis has generated enormous excitement with **14,000+ GitHub stars** in its first weeks. It claims **43 million FPS** for a Franka arm on a single RTX 4090 — 10–80× faster than Isaac Gym. Its custom-built universal physics engine integrates rigid body dynamics, MPM (granular materials), SPH (fluids), FEM (deformables), and PBD solvers in a unified, fully differentiable framework. Built on Taichi for cross-platform GPU compilation (NVIDIA, AMD, Apple Metal, CPU). Native ray-tracing rendering. Installation: `pip install genesis-world`.

The catch: Genesis is still **beta software with no VR support, no ROS2 integration, and limited validation** for manipulation contact physics. The promised generative data engine (natural language to simulation worlds) is not yet open-sourced. MJCF and URDF import are supported. For your use case, Genesis is worth monitoring closely but not ready as a primary platform today.

### CoppeliaSim: five physics engines but a licensing caveat

CoppeliaSim (V4.10.0, May 2025) is unique in offering **five switchable physics engines**: MuJoCo, Bullet, ODE, Newton, and Vortex Dynamics. Its MuJoCo integration was upgraded to V3.2.6 in January 2025, delivering 2–10× speed improvements. The VR Toolbox (open-source, HTC Vive support via OpenVR) enables VR teleoperation. RLBench, the 100+ task manipulation benchmark used by PerAct and RVT, is built on CoppeliaSim via PyRep. ROS2 is supported through the simROS2 plugin. The GUI enables intuitive scene building.

Critical issues: **the Edu license is restricted to university students/teachers only** — research labs and companies need the ~$2,380/year Pro license. PyRep/RLBench are pinned to the ancient V4.1.0, creating a gap with modern features. No GPU-accelerated physics. Rendering is functional but dated.

### Webots: easiest to learn but limited for IL

Webots R2025a (February 2025) is fully open-source (Apache 2.0) and designed for **maximum ease of use**. Cross-platform (Windows, Linux, macOS including Apple Silicon), deterministic simulation, modern Qt GUI, and excellent tutorials make it the most beginner-friendly option. The webots_ros2 package provides solid ROS2 integration for Humble, Jazzy, and Kilted. Mobile manipulators like TIAGo are supported, and 750+ universities use it worldwide.

Limitations: single ODE-based physics engine, no GPU acceleration, limited rendering quality (OpenGL 3.3), **negligible IL/RL research ecosystem**, and no VR toolbox. Webots is excellent for education and ROS2 prototyping but not for serious imitation learning research.

### PyBullet: a legacy simulator in decline

PyBullet (3.2.7, January 2025) is **effectively unmaintained**. Its creator Erwin Coumans moved to NVIDIA, and innovations now flow into Isaac Sim rather than back to Bullet. No GPU-parallel simulation, single-threaded CPU physics, basic OpenGL rendering, and documentation limited to a single Google Doc. It uniquely has **built-in VR support for HTC Vive and Oculus Rift**, but this dates from 2017–2018. Still gets ~23,000 weekly PyPI downloads from legacy projects, but **not recommended for new projects** when MuJoCo is free and vastly superior.

### Game engine approaches: Unity and Unreal

**Unity + Robotics Hub** has excellent native VR/XR support (Meta Quest, OpenXR, SteamVR) and powerful UI building tools. However, **the Unity Robotics Hub appears effectively abandoned** — multiple forum threads from 2024 report no team activity for 1–2 years. The ROS-TCP-Connector last saw significant updates in 2022. Unity's C#-centric workflow clashes with robotics researchers' Python preference. ML-Agents supports basic IL but lacks ACT/Diffusion Policy integration.

**Unreal Engine 5** offers the **best photorealistic rendering** (Nanite, Lumen, DLSS) and native VR support. However, the robotics ecosystem is fragmented across competing forks: Project AirSim (drones/vehicles), Colosseum, Cosys-AirSim. **None focus on manipulation tasks.** Complex C++ build process, no parallel physics simulation, and no Python-native workflow make UE5 impractical for your use case.

### Embodied AI simulators: navigation-focused

**Habitat 3.0 (Meta AI)** achieves 8,000+ steps/sec and excels at **social mobile manipulation** — robot-humanoid collaboration tasks using Fetch, Spot, and Stretch robots in photorealistic 3D-scanned environments (HM3D: 1,000 scans). Habitat 3.0 includes VR headset support for human-in-the-loop evaluation. However, Bullet physics is not ideal for fine manipulation, there's **no ROS2 integration**, custom object/scene creation is difficult, and it uses a proprietary training framework.

**AI2-THOR** provides 200+ artist-built scenes and ManipulaTHOR for mobile manipulation research. ProcTHOR generates 10,000+ procedural houses. Easy to install (`pip install ai2thor`), but physics are partially abstracted ("magic" teleport-style actions), **no URDF import, no VR support, no ROS2**. Best for high-level embodied AI tasks rather than contact-rich manipulation.

### RAVENS and RLBench: benchmark suites, not full simulators

**RAVENS** (Google Research, 2020) is **archived** — a PyBullet-based tabletop benchmark for Transporter Networks with 10 pick-and-place tasks. No GPU acceleration, no VR, no mobile manipulation, no ROS2. Not relevant for your use case.

**RLBench** (Imperial College, 2020) provides 100+ manipulation tasks on CoppeliaSim, widely used by PerAct and RVT. The community RLBench_ACT project (2024) added ACT and Diffusion Policy support. But it's pinned to CoppeliaSim V4.1.0, CPU-only, and limited to tabletop manipulation with fixed robot arms.

### RFUniverse and SAPIEN: niche but notable

**RFUniverse** (Shanghai Jiao Tong University, v0.30.3) is Unity-based with unique **multiphysics capabilities** (fluid, cloth, heat transfer) and **native VR support via SteamVR**. Meta Quest can connect via Quest Link. Used in garment folding (UniFolding) and caregiving research. Smaller community (~145 stars), partially Chinese documentation, no GPU-parallel simulation.

**SAPIEN 3** (UCSD) is primarily the engine underneath ManiSkill3. PhysX 5 GPU physics, Vulkan rendering, and an entity-component architecture. Most users interact through ManiSkill rather than SAPIEN directly. No native VR, ROS2, or MJCF support.

## The VR teleoperation + imitation learning landscape in 2025

The ecosystem for collecting VR demonstrations and training IL policies has matured significantly. Here are the key tools bridging Meta Quest to robot learning:

- **Open-Teach** (NYU, 2024): Built specifically around **Meta Quest 3** ($500). Supports 38 tasks across Franka, xArm, Jaco, Allegro Hand, and **Hello Stretch mobile manipulator** at up to 90 Hz. Works with real robots and compatible with policy learning pipelines.
- **phospho teleoperation**: A **Meta Store app** for Quest 2/Pro/3/3S that records teleoperation data directly in **LeRobot v2 format** and auto-uploads to HuggingFace. The simplest VR-to-IL path available.
- **Unitree xr_teleoperate**: Supports Meta Quest 3, Apple Vision Pro, and PICO 4 Ultra for teleoperation in both **Isaac Lab simulation and real robots**, with a recording mode that outputs LeRobot-compatible data.
- **Open-TeleVision** (MIT/UCSD, 2024): Stereoscopic VR teleoperation demonstrated coast-to-coast over internet. Works with Apple Vision Pro for humanoid control. ACT policy training validated.

The trend is clear: **VR teleoperation increasingly targets real robots (or Isaac Lab) rather than lightweight simulators**, with data flowing into LeRobot format for training. The most practical pipeline is VR teleop on real hardware → LeRobot dataset → train ACT/Diffusion Policy → deploy. Simulation serves as a supplementary training and evaluation environment.

## How LeRobot connects everything

**Hugging Face LeRobot v0.4.0** has become the unifying framework for imitation learning, and its supported simulator list drives practical choices:

| Environment | Backend | Integration | Tasks |
|---|---|---|---|
| gym-aloha | MuJoCo | Native | Transfer Cube, Insertion |
| LIBERO | Robosuite/MuJoCo | Native (v0.4.0) | 130+ language-conditioned |
| Meta-World | MuJoCo | Native (v0.4.0) | 50+ manipulation |
| gym_hil | MuJoCo | Native | Franka Panda HIL |
| Isaac Lab-Arena | Isaac Sim/PhysX | Native (EnvHub) | GR00T evaluation |

LeRobot supports **ACT, Diffusion Policy, π0/π0.5, SmolVLA, and GR00T N1.5** policies. The data format (LeRobotDataset v3.0 — Parquet + MP4) is becoming the standard for IL dataset sharing on HuggingFace Hub. **Every natively supported simulator uses MuJoCo** except Isaac Lab-Arena.

## Ranked recommendations for your specific use case

Your requirements: mobile manipulator + VR teleop (Meta Quest 3S) + imitation learning (ACT, Diffusion Policy, RT-2) + ROS2 + RTX Pro 6000 + easier than Isaac Sim.

### Recommendation 1: MuJoCo + LeRobot + Open-Teach + Gazebo (Best practical stack)

**Why**: MuJoCo provides the best manipulation physics and is the native platform for ACT and Diffusion Policy. LeRobot unifies training across policies. Open-Teach gives you Meta Quest 3 teleoperation for data collection (primarily on real hardware, with sim evaluation on MuJoCo environments). Gazebo handles ROS2 integration and mobile base testing. Your RTX Pro 6000 can run MJX/MuJoCo Warp for GPU-accelerated training. This stack is **dramatically easier to set up** than Isaac Sim — `pip install mujoco lerobot` gets you started in minutes.

**Workflow**: Design mobile manipulator URDF → test in Gazebo with ROS2/Nav2/MoveIt → collect VR demonstrations with Open-Teach on real robot (or phospho Meta Quest app) → store in LeRobot format on HuggingFace → train ACT/Diffusion Policy via LeRobot → evaluate in MuJoCo sim environments → deploy via ROS2.

**Gaps**: No photorealistic rendering in MuJoCo; VR teleop is on real hardware not in sim; requires managing multiple tools.

### Recommendation 2: OmniGibson + TeleMoMa (Most integrated VR+IL pipeline)

**Why**: The only simulator with a battle-tested VR teleoperation → demonstration collection → IL training pipeline in a single package. TeleMoMa supports Meta Quest controllers. ACT and Diffusion Policy are validated. Four mobile manipulators included. RTX ray-traced rendering via your RTX Pro 6000. ROS2 available through Isaac Sim bridge.

**Caveat**: Built on Isaac Sim/Omniverse, which you found too difficult. OmniGibson's Python API is cleaner than raw Isaac Sim, but the underlying installation and dependency management remain heavy. Expect similar GPU memory and complexity issues.

### Recommendation 3: Isaac Lab v2.3 (Structured Isaac Sim with growing VR)

**Why**: If you want to give the NVIDIA ecosystem another chance, Isaac Lab's abstraction layer is **significantly easier than standalone Isaac Sim**. Template generators, Direct workflow mode, and comprehensive tutorials reduce the learning curve. Your RTX Pro 6000 is explicitly the "Best" tier GPU. Growing VR support (Vive, Manus gloves, CloudXR). LeRobot integration via Isaac Lab-Arena. Newton physics engine co-developed with DeepMind. The largest robot model library (16+ morphologies).

**Caveat**: Still requires full Omniverse installation. 2–3× more GPU memory than ManiSkill3. Steeper learning curve than MuJoCo-based alternatives.

### Recommendation 4: ManiSkill3 + custom VR bridge (Best for GPU-accelerated IL training)

**Why**: If training speed is your priority, ManiSkill3's **30,000+ FPS** with 2–3× less GPU memory than Isaac Lab is compelling. Diffusion Policy and VLA baselines included. Mobile manipulator tasks supported. Fully open-source (Apache 2.0). Your RTX Pro 6000 would deliver exceptional performance.

**Gaps**: No VR support (would need custom OpenXR bridge), no ROS2 integration, still in beta, rasterized rendering only.

### Recommendation 5: RFUniverse (Best native VR in a lightweight simulator)

**Why**: Unity-based with **native SteamVR support**, meaning Meta Quest 3S connects via Quest Link. VR demonstration collection demonstrated in published research. Unique multiphysics (fluids, cloth, heat). ROS2 via RF_ROS module. Easier to set up than Isaac Sim.

**Gaps**: Smaller community (~145 stars), partially Chinese documentation, no GPU-parallel simulation, no established ACT/Diffusion Policy pipeline.

## What to avoid and why

**PyBullet**: Effectively unmaintained since 2021. Its VR support is legacy (HTC Vive/Oculus Rift from 2017). MuJoCo is strictly superior in every dimension that matters for manipulation research. Skip it.

**RAVENS**: Archived. Ten tabletop tasks, no mobile manipulation, no VR. Historically important but superseded.

**Unity Robotics Hub**: The robotics-specific tooling appears abandoned. Unity's VR platform is excellent, but without active robotics integration, you'd be building everything from scratch in C#.

**Unreal Engine**: Best rendering in the industry but the robotics ecosystem is fragmented across competing forks focused on drones/vehicles, not manipulation. The C++ build complexity is worse than Isaac Sim.

**AI2-THOR / Habitat**: Designed for embodied AI navigation research with abstracted physics. Neither supports custom URDF import well, and their manipulation capabilities are limited compared to MuJoCo-based alternatives. No ROS2 integration.

## The emerging convergence to watch

The robotics simulation landscape is consolidating around two physics backends: **MuJoCo** (academic IL/manipulation research) and **PhysX** (NVIDIA ecosystem, GPU-parallel RL). The March 2025 announcement of **MuJoCo Warp** — a joint DeepMind+NVIDIA project — signals these worlds are merging. Isaac Lab now offers a MuJoCo-Warp-powered API alongside PhysX. Genesis builds its own engine but supports both MJCF and URDF import.

Meanwhile, **LeRobot is becoming the standard IL training framework**, and its simulator support list (all MuJoCo-based plus Isaac Lab-Arena) effectively determines which simulators matter for imitation learning. If a simulator isn't in LeRobot's ecosystem, adopting it for IL research means writing custom integration code.

For your specific combination of needs, the pragmatic 2025 approach is to **stop looking for one simulator that does everything** and instead build a modular stack: MuJoCo for physics and IL training, Gazebo for ROS2 and mobile base control, Open-Teach or phospho for Meta Quest VR teleoperation, and LeRobot as the glue connecting demonstrations to policy training. This stack is easier than Isaac Sim, proven in recent research, and directly compatible with ACT and Diffusion Policy out of the box.
